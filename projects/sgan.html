PROJECT 02 · SEMI-SUPERVISED LEARNING

SGAN for
smart microscopy.

Learning cell-cycle phenotypes from a small labelled set and a larger pool of unlabelled images.

Research question

How can we reduce the annotation burden in high-content microscopy while maintaining reliable cell-phase classification?

Method

The project investigates a semi-supervised generative adversarial network (SGAN) with a shared discriminator for supervised phenotype classification and unsupervised real/fake discrimination. The model is trained using labelled and unlabelled microscopy images, with data augmentation and experiments that vary the number of labelled examples per class.

Experimental setting

The initial study focuses on five Mitocheck phenotypes: Anaphase, Apoptosis, Interphase, Metaphase and Prometaphase. Experiments examine low-label regimes and the contribution of additional unlabelled images.

Reported result

In the reported Mitocheck experiments, the SGAN reached approximately 93 ± 2% accuracy using 80 labelled images per class and 600 unlabelled images. This is a dataset- and protocol-specific result, not a claim of general performance across microscopy domains.

Next questions

Current methodological questions include transfer to new imaging conditions, class imbalance, robustness to domain shift and comparison with alternative semi-supervised and self-supervised representation-learning strategies.

Publication status: Manuscript in progress / under review. A public preprint or article link will be added when available.
