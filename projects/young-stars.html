PROJECT 03 · TIME-DOMAIN ASTROPHYSICS

Young stars &
nascent planets.

Using stellar variability as a window into early planetary-system evolution.

Scientific motivation

Young stellar systems are dynamic environments. Accretion, magnetic activity, circumstellar material, rotation and orbital interactions can all imprint structure on observed light curves. Understanding these signals is essential for identifying and characterising young planetary systems.

Approach

This research direction combines time-series analysis, photometric surveys and machine learning to identify variability patterns in young stars. TESS full-frame images provide high-cadence photometric data that can be used to search for transient and periodic behaviours across large stellar samples.

A very young planet around CI Tau

In work published in 2024, our team reported evidence for a massive, close-in planet around the approximately two-million-year-old classical T Tauri star CI Tau. The study combined high-resolution spectroscopy with multi-wavelength ground-based photometry and space-based Kepler photometry. The reported planet has a mass of about 3.6 Jupiter masses and an orbital period of approximately 25 days.

I named this planet Uma in memory of my late mother. Uma is a personal name I chose for it; it is not the planet’s official astronomical designation, which remains associated with the CI Tau system.

Research questions

  • How can variability classes be distinguished robustly in young stellar populations?
  • Can machine learning identify rare or evolving light-curve morphologies?
  • Which signals are consistent with accretion, circumstellar structures or planet-related phenomena?
  • How can survey-scale classification remain interpretable and scientifically validated?

Background

My earlier astrophysics work focused on evolved stars, RV Tauri variables, post-AGB binaries and spectroscopic variability. This provides a foundation in stellar evolution, binary validation and time-domain analysis that informs the broader study of variable stars.

Project scope: Research direction spanning survey analysis, variability classification and young exoplanet detection.
