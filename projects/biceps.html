PROJECT 01 · COMPUTATIONAL MICROSCOPY

BICEPs.

Interpretable, feature-based cell-phase classification for quantitative microscopy.

Research question

Can carefully engineered image descriptors and transparent statistical learning provide robust cell-cycle classification without relying entirely on large, computationally expensive deep neural networks?

Approach

BICEPs explores a structured pipeline: image-level feature extraction, statistical feature ranking, dimensionality reduction and neighbourhood-based classification. The current work uses features describing object morphology, intensity distributions and spatial-frequency structure. It is designed to make the evidence behind a prediction easier to inspect.

Current research directions

  • Feature engineering for cellular morphology and texture.
  • Feature selection with statistical testing and false-discovery-rate control.
  • Evaluation across cell-cycle datasets, including Mitocheck and Jurkat.
  • Analysis of class imbalance, domain shift and embedding-space limitations.
  • Comparison of interpretable classifiers with deep-learning baselines.

Why it matters

Microscopy datasets are often small, imbalanced and expensive to annotate. Interpretable feature-based methods can offer a useful baseline, reveal which image properties drive classification and help identify where more expressive representation learning is genuinely needed.

Project status: Research and method development. Results and software links will be added as they are released.
